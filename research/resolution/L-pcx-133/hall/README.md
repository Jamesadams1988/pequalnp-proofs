# Three pigeons in two holes

The binary pigeonhole formula in L-pcx-133 assigns five labelled pigeons to four labelled holes. For each choice of three pigeons and two holes, a Hall clause says those three pigeons cannot all occupy the selected two holes. Each pair of two-bit hole labels is an affine line over GF(2), so this is a disjunction of three linear equations in Res(⊕).

**Twenty Hall clauses are necessary and sufficient to exclude all assignments.** There are exactly three minimum families. Each partitions the holes into two pairs and uses all ten pigeon triples for each pair. The three partitions are 01|23, 02|13, and 03|12, with decimal hole labels 0–3.

There are 240 assignments with exactly one colliding pair. Choose the colliding pigeons in 10 ways, their hole in 4 ways, and biject the remaining three pigeons to the remaining holes in 6 ways. A fixed Hall clause excludes 12 of these assignments. The duplicated pigeons are two of its three, their shared hole is one of its two, and the two pigeons outside the triple occupy the remaining two holes in either order. A family covering all 240 therefore has at least 20 members. A minimum family covers these assignments without overlap. The replay enumerates all such exact covers and then checks each on all 1,024 assignments.

This yields a conditional 159-line lower bound for a two-phase proof. Suppose each first-phase Hall clause has its own derivation from original axioms, sharing only original axiom lines with other such derivations. Suppose the final derivation then uses only Hall outputs as premises. At least 20 distinct Hall premises must contribute to the contradiction. For each Hall clause, its 12 unique-collision assignments certify that six distinct original axioms are indispensable. Connecting six premises takes at least five binary inference lines; weakening is unary. The final contradiction requires at least 19 binary inference lines, and every full refutation uses all 40 original axioms. The sum is 40 + 20 × 5 + 19 = 159. Proofs that share derived lines across first-phase derivations or use original axioms again in the final phase fall outside this bound. The unrestricted 127-line question remains open in this calculation.

Run `python3 verify.py` from this directory. The replay uses only the Python standard library, exact integer masks, and an exhaustive backtracking check.
