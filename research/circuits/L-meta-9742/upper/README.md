# Optimal star circuits for L-meta-9742

Research credit belongs to James Adams. For the listed 26-vertex graph with 51 edges, **63 gates and depth seven are simultaneously optimal** in the following model. Raw inputs feed arbitrary shared binary OR circuits. A product is an AND of two resulting wires, including raw input wires. The output is an arbitrary shared binary OR circuit over the products. Constants, copies and unused gates are permitted. The lower bounds apply to this model. The native circuit in [Upper63.lean](Upper63.lean) gives the matching upper bound, with a Lean proof on all assignments.

## Size and equality

Discard gates outside the output cone, simplify constants, and suppress copies, including ORs with singleton support. A pure wire accepts the OR of some set of variables. Since every product reaching the output implies the edge function, its two supports are disjoint and their complete bipartite graph is contained in the listed graph. The graph has no four-cycle, so one support is a singleton. Orient that vertex as the center, choosing either endpoint for a one-edge product.

Let k count the remaining products. For each center v, let rᵥ count its products and let dᵥ be the number of distinct vertices in the union of their leaf supports. All edges are covered, so ∑dᵥ ≥ 51 and ∑rᵥ = k. To assemble the rᵥ leaf wires from dᵥ sources takes at least dᵥ−rᵥ preprocessing ORs. Indeed, the union of their ancestor graphs has at most rᵥ weak components. Starting with dᵥ sources, one binary gate can reduce the component count by at most one. No retained preprocessing OR can be charged to two centers. Its support would give two common neighbors of distinct centers. The output OR circuit requires at least k−1 gates by the same component count. Including the k products gives at least 51+k−1 gates.

The centers form a vertex cover. The exact minimum cover size is 13, and the two shores are its only solutions. For each of the 8,192 subsets of the point shore, the lines adjacent to an unchosen point are precisely the forced lines. Testing which of these completed covers are inclusion-minimal proves both statements. If the circuit has exactly 63 gates, it therefore has one product per vertex of a complete shore. Every product must be the full star at its center, since that center has no competitor for any incident edge. Within each star's OR ancestry the component bound is tight, forcing a binary tree. The same holds in the output suffix. Thus **no computed gate output is shared** in an optimal pruned circuit. Raw inputs may be reused. Up to swapping operands and reordering independent gate evaluations, the optimal circuits are exactly the binary bracketings of these full stars and of their output disjunction. For labeled variables there are 2·3·15¹²·23!! = 246,181,331,996,538,903,808,593,750. Each shore has one three-leaf and twelve four-leaf stars. A commutative binary tree on k labeled leaves has (2k−3)!! forms, by inserting the last leaf above one of the previous tree’s nodes.

More generally, fix the centers of the nonzero products that reach the output, orienting any one-edge product to a chosen endpoint. Fixing these distinct centers to any vertex cover C gives exact minimum size 51+|C|−1. For the upper bound, match each center to a different incident edge, then assign each remaining edge to one incident center. Such a matching exists by Hall's theorem: a subset of t centers has at least 3t incident edge ends, with at most two ends per edge. This partitions the edges into |C| nonempty stars. Compute each with one product and one fewer OR than it has leaves, then OR the results. In particular, requiring at least one vertex of each shore to be absent from the centers raises the exact minimum to **65**. There are exactly two smallest such center sets, both of size 15. The checker prints them.

## Depth

Any OR signal with r distinct raw inputs has depth at least ⌈log₂ r⌉, including when intermediates are shared. A useful product of depth d covers at most 2^(d−1) edges since its nontrivial side has that many potential inputs. For every wire in the output OR circuit, twice the number of accepted edge inputs is consequently at most two raised to that wire's depth. This inequality propagates through binary OR because the union of its children's accepted edges has size at most the sum. At the output, 2·51 > 2⁶, proving depth at least seven throughout the model. A balanced OR for each star followed by a balanced OR of the thirteen products attains depth seven. More precisely, suppose an optimal-size circuit uses an unbalanced bracketing on r of its twelve four-leaf stars. These r products have depth four and the other thirteen minus r have depth three. The least output depth obtainable by choosing the final OR tree is seven for r ≤ 3 and eight for r ≥ 4. To see this, each product of depth h must be placed at output-tree distance at most D−h. The binary prefix-code criterion is ∑2ʰ ≤ 2ᴰ, and here the sum is 104+8r. A prefix code satisfying these distance bounds gives the suffix tree after contracting unary branches.

## Prescribed first gate

In a size-63 circuit each star has at least three leaves, so the first gate must be a binary OR of two distinct neighbors of some center. Conversely any such pair can be combined first by choosing that center's shore and a corresponding bracketing of its star. Exactly 300 ordered binary ORs qualify, and each can start a depth-seven optimum as well. The remaining 1,106 native first gates, counting AND and OR with input-list lengths zero, one or two drawn from the 26 raw wires, have exact restricted minimum **64**. They cannot start a size-63 circuit and can be prepended as a dead gate to the size-63 witness with the later gates renumbered. This classification also allows repeated operands.
