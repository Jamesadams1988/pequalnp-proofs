# Exact size of a restricted two-terminal incidence circuit

Let G be the point-line incidence graph with 13 points and 13 lines, joined when the index sum modulo 13 belongs to {0,1,3,9}. Its 52 edges all have endpoints of degree four. Any two vertices have at most one common neighbor. The edges from point p to line −p form a 13-edge matching.

For a set of true input vertices, let f be true precisely when both endpoints of at least one edge are true. The positive and negative tests of L-meta-1988 determine f among monotone functions. Indeed, four points meet at least 4·4 − 6 = 10 lines by the pairwise intersection property. An independent set therefore has at most three points or at most three lines. It extends to a tested negative assignment by retaining the smaller side and adding every nonneighbor on the other side. Symmetry of the incidence relation justifies the transposed tests. Each positive assignment contains a tested edge.

## The circuit model

Allow an arbitrary fan-in-two OR circuit on the raw variables. A product gate takes the AND of two such pure OR signals, which can include raw variables. Require each product, on its own, to imply f. An arbitrary fan-in-two OR circuit then combines the products and any pure signals, including raw inputs. The result is the free conjunction of up to two designated wires. Any wire can fan out or be designated. The size counts all the gates except the free conjunction. Constants, unary gates and invalid reads with false default can be simplified or removed without increasing size. In particular a terminal identically true reduces the conjunction to its other terminal. Disconnected gates can only increase size.

**The minimum size is 64.** This covers shared pure signals, shared product outputs, and shared postprocessing. Circuits with nested product gates or with individual product gates accepting a graph false input fall outside the lower bound below.

## Construction

Form F as the OR of the twelve complete point stars centered at points 1 through 12. Each star uses three OR gates for its line neighbors and one AND with its point. Combining the twelve values costs eleven more OR gates, so F costs 59 gates. Let x be point 0 and let Y be the OR of its four line neighbors, costing three gates. Use two more gates for F ∨ x and F ∨ Y. Their conjunction equals F ∨ (x ∧ Y), precisely f. Each output by itself is true on a singleton independent set.

Run `python3 verify.py` in this directory. It checks the finite geometry, both predecessor indices of every gate, and the conjunction at wire indices 88 and 89 on all 67,108,864 assignments. It uses one machine-independent Python integer to evaluate the 8192 line assignments in parallel for each of the 8192 point assignments.

The Lean file `Upper64.lean` imports `PEqualNP.Defs`. From a built project checkout run `lake env lean /absolute/path/to/Upper64.lean`. The theorem `free_output_spec` identifies the actual free conjunction with the existence of an active edge satisfying the modular incidence predicate on every 26-bit input. The file also proves the size and gate basis. The lower bound below is a mathematical proof.

## Lower bound

Discard constants, copies, gates with unused outputs, and products identically false. A surviving pure signal is a disjunction of a nonempty set of variables, called its support. A product of pure signals with supports A and B may imply f only if those supports are disjoint, since otherwise the product would accept a singleton. Every pair across A and B must be an edge, by the two-vertex assignment. The resulting complete bipartite graph is a star since G has no four-cycle. Thus each real product covers at most four edges. Choose either endpoint as the center of a one-edge star.

Call OR gates with a product ancestor postprocessing gates. An edge entering this postprocessing from the pure OR circuit carries a pure boundary signal. Also count a designated pure wire itself as a boundary signal. For terminal i, let Qᵢ be the union of supports of the boundary signals that reach it through postprocessing OR gates alone, with a path of length zero allowed. Label the number of distinct such signals hᵢ. A boundary signal cannot reach both terminals since it is nonempty and would make their conjunction accept a singleton.

We will use a counting observation. For each star center v, collect rᵥ pure leaf-side signals, all supported inside its neighborhood, with union Uᵥ. The pure preprocessing circuit has at least the sum over v of |Uᵥ| − rᵥ OR gates. To see this, contract copies and wires with singleton support onto their raw variable. In the union of the ancestor DAGs of these rᵥ signals, there are |Uᵥ| input vertices, at most rᵥ connected components, and at most two incoming edges at each gate. Counting edges in the underlying undirected graph gives at least |Uᵥ| − rᵥ gates. Every counted gate has support of size at least two inside the neighborhood of v. It cannot occur in the count for another center since two neighborhoods intersect in at most one vertex. Repeated leaf-side signals in the list for a fixed v only weaken the bound.

With just one designated wire, or if either Qᵢ is empty, the terminal with no bypass computes f on its own. In the single-terminal case any nonempty bypass would incorrectly accept a singleton. A terminal without a bypass is an OR of sound products and must accept each of the 52 edge assignments. The k products reaching it therefore cover every edge, so k ≥ 13. Apply the counting observation to their leaf-side signals. Their union sizes sum to at least 52 since their stars cover all edges, giving at least 52 − k preprocessing gates. Combining k products into this one terminal takes at least k − 1 further OR gates by the same edge count. Together with the k products this yields at least 51 + k ≥ 64. An empty terminal list is constantly true and fails the negative tests.

Suppose now both Qᵢ are nonempty. On every assignment satisfying both pure disjunctions over Q₁ and Q₂, both terminals accept. Their cross product is therefore a sound virtual star by the same singleton and two-vertex argument. Relabel so Q₁ = {c} and Q₂ is a nonempty subset of its at most four neighbors. The k real products together with this virtual star cover every edge. Indeed, if no real product is active on an edge assignment, both terminals must accept via their respective bypasses. In particular k ≥ 12.

For each real star use its single leaf-side signal in the counting observation. For the virtual star centered at c also use the h₂ boundary signals furnishing Q₂ as leaf-side signals. The 52 covered edges give a sum of union sizes at least 52. The sum of the rᵥ is k + h₂. The pure preprocessing therefore takes at least 52 − k − h₂ gates.

The postprocessing DAG has k + h₁ + h₂ distinct sources, comprising all real products and both sets of boundary signals. These are distinct circuit wires even if some happen to compute the same function. Every source and postprocessing gate reaches at least one terminal, so the underlying graph has at most two components. If it has t components and g OR gates, there are at most 2g edges and at least k + h₁ + h₂ + g − t edges. Consequently g ≥ k + h₁ + h₂ − t. This and the preprocessing bound show the total size is at least 52 + k + h₁ − t. Since h₁ ≥ 1 and t ≤ 2, this proves 64 when k ≥ 13.

If k = 12, the thirteen real and virtual stars must partition all 52 edges into sets of exactly four. The matching of size thirteen has an edge avoiding the at most five vertices of Q₁ ∪ Q₂. On this assignment both terminals accept with no bypass, and exactly one real product is true. That product must reach both terminals. Their postprocessing DAG is therefore connected, so t = 1 and the total is at least 52 + 12 + 1 − 1 = 64.

## Regular graphs

The proof gives the same exact formula for every d-regular, four-cycle-free bipartite graph with n vertices per side, d ≥ 1 and n > d + 1. Such a graph has a perfect matching by Hall's theorem, since the d|S| edges out of S have at most d endpoints at each vertex of its neighborhood. Write E = nd. Each star covers at most d edges. The two-terminal lower bound is E + k + h₁ − t, with k ≥ n − 1. When k = n − 1, the n stars partition the E edges and some matching edge avoids the at most d + 1 bypass vertices. This forces t = 1. For the upper bound, take the n − 1 entire stars on one side except a chosen vertex, then apply the two-terminal construction to that last star. The exact size is E + n − 1.
