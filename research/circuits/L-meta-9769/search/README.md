# Signed disjunctions and safe products

The 42-input graph in L-meta-9769 needs exactly **124 gates** in the following circuit class. The input stage may share binary OR gates freely and may use complemented individual input variables. The middle stage consists of binary AND gates whose two operands come from that input stage or its literals. Each product must be safe on **every** input, meaning that when the product is true, an edge is present. The output stage may use any fan-in-at-most-two AND/OR/NOT circuit on the product outputs. It has no direct access to primary inputs or earlier preprocessing wires. The cost counts gates, including negations. The lower bound even survives free access to the negated input literals, although the equality statement about NOT gates below uses their ordinary charged cost.

This extends the shared-union star accounting used on the earlier L-meta-9802 graph to signed literals and arbitrary Boolean postprocessing on the new graph. The unrestricted desk model also permits unsafe products and interleaving gate types.

## Lower bound

1. **Erase the negative literals.** Each product is the conjunction of two disjunctions of literals. On any input where it is true, pick one true literal from each side. If the two literals include at most one positive variable, an assignment with at most that one variable selected also satisfies both. This violates safety. Complementary literals cannot both have been true. Thus every successful pair of choices consists of positive endpoints of an edge. Erasing every negative literal from the shared input OR network preserves every product's function, with zero gates needed to represent an empty support for purposes of this accounting.

2. **Find the centers.** A nonempty product now has positive supports $A,B$ with every cross pair an edge. They are disjoint since the graph has no loops. They lie on opposite sides since the graph is bipartite. The literal graph has no four-cycle, so one support is a singleton. Choose that vertex as center, breaking ties arbitrarily for one-edge products. Restrict attention to nonempty product gates reachable backwards from the final output, and let $k$ count these distinct gate IDs. On the empty assignment all products are false. On each two-endpoint edge assignment the final answer is different, so at least one reachable nonempty product covers that edge. This holds for any Boolean output stage. A 21-edge matching shows $k\ge21$.

3. **Count the shared preprocessing gates.** For a center $c$, let $r_c$ be its number of products and $d_c$ the number of distinct leaves in their union. In each preprocessing subgraph that builds a leaf operand, every positive support is contained in the neighborhood of $c$. Bypass empty and singleton supports in this union-only network. Gates with at least two distinct positive inputs in their support can belong to at most one center, because two common neighbors would form a four-cycle. For one center, joining its $r_c$ operand outputs with $r_c-1$ hypothetical ORs computes the disjunction of all $d_c$ leaves. Connecting $d_c$ distinct inputs to one output needs at least $d_c-1$ binary gates. Thus at least $d_c-r_c$ distinct actual ORs can be charged to $c$. Summing over centers gives at least $104-k$ input ORs since every edge is covered.

4. **Join the products.** The backwards cone of an output depending syntactically on $k$ distinct product wires contains at least $k-1$ binary gates in the output stage. This follows by expanding the output backwards, increasing the number of pending wires by at most one per binary gate. Unary gates add no wires. Adding the disjoint stage costs gives at least $(104-k)+k+(k-1)=103+k\ge124$. The 21 row stars attain the bound using 83 input ORs, 21 products and 20 output ORs.

## Equality and mixed centers

For a set $S$ of omitted point centers, every neighbor of $S$ is a forced line center. The smallest completion has $21-|S|+|N(S)|$ centers. The script enumerates all $2^{21}$ choices of $S$. Exactly two covers attain 21 centers, one consisting of every point and the other of every line. Therefore every 124-gate circuit in this class uses precisely one product for each vertex of one side. Each product covers its center's full neighborhood. All 124 gates have already been charged as binary gates, so equality leaves no charged NOT gates.

The **127-gate bound is for proper mixed center sets**. Such sets use at least one center on each shore **and omit at least one vertex on each shore**. Their minimum center count is 24, implying at least 127 gates. There are exactly two minimum proper mixed center sets. Every center in either set has an edge that no other selected center covers. Assign each edge to one of its selected endpoints. The resulting 24 nonempty stars partition the 104 edges, giving 104 gates to form the stars and 23 OR gates to join them. Thus 127 is also attained for proper mixed centers.

If a mixed cover may use an entire shore, the sharp cost is **125**. Keep all 21 point centers, select one additional line center, and assign two of its incident edges to its star. Every point retains at least three edges, and the line gets two, so all 22 centers are unambiguous. The 104 edges partition into 22 stars and 21 final ORs join them. The equality characterization above rules out 124 for these mixed covers.
