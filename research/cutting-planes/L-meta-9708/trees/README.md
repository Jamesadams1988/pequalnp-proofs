# Edge-disjoint trees of clique-capacity cuts

Consider one hole with `k` pigeon variables. A pair clause gives a two-point capacity `xᵢ+xⱼ ≤ 1`. In this proof model a node derives the capacity of its vertex set by taking a nonnegative integer weighted sum of previously derived clique capacities and dividing the sum once. The coefficient before division must be the same positive integer on each vertex. The input capacities are on proper subsets with at least two points. Different children have disjoint sets of pair-clause premises. Multiplying a line counts as an instruction, and adding a line to itself also counts as an instruction.

Every capacity requires all the pair clauses internal to its vertex set. Otherwise the Boolean assignment that puts one on the two ends of a missing pair and zero elsewhere satisfies those premises and violates the capacity. Consequently the child vertex sets partition the parent's pairs, with any two children meeting in at most one point.

Suppose `k ≥ 4` and every child leaves at least two points outside. If every child has at most two points, choose any four points. Otherwise choose a child `A` of size at least three and two outside points `p,q`. Their joining child `D` meets `A` in at most one point. Select distinct `a,b` in `A` outside `D`. Every triple of the four points `a,b,p,q` contains either the pair `a,b` or the pair `p,q`. The unique child containing the first pair is `A`, and the unique child containing the second is `D`. Neither contains a third selected point.

Assigning one half to the four selected points and zero to the others satisfies all child capacities and gives total occupancy two. If the weighted sum has coefficient `d > 0` on every vertex, its right-hand side must therefore be at least `2d`. Dividing by `d` cannot produce capacity one. A successful node must have a child of size `k−1`. Each other child consists of the missing vertex and one of the `k−1` original vertices. The same pattern at `k=3` consists of three pair clauses.

Write `m=k−1`, `c` for the weight of the large child, and `wᵢ` for the edge weights. Equality of the coefficients gives `c+wᵢ=d` at every old vertex and `Σᵢ wᵢ=d` at the new vertex. Thus all `wᵢ` equal some `t>0`, `d=mt`, and `c=(m−1)t`. For every `k≥4` the large child and a star edge have different weights. A positive unweighted addition tree using each child once cannot realize these coefficients. At least one extra scaling or repeated-input addition is required at each such node.

For `k` pigeons the tree uses `k(k−1)/2` pair premises and at least one fewer binary additions to connect them. It requires `k−2` divisions and `k−3` further scalings or repeated-input additions for `k≥3`. The ordinary chain of capacities meets this bound, so the minimum length in this model is `k²+k−6`. At `k=6` it is 36. Five independent capacity trees, six pigeon premises and ten additions give exactly 196 lines. This does not cover arbitrary CP derivations. Intermediate inequalities may have unequal coefficients or a larger right-hand side, sources can be shared across branches, and a global proof may mix holes.

`CliqueTree.lean` formalizes the quadrangle construction, the weighted obstruction and the forced star multiplicities. The instruction count above is a mathematical corollary in the explicitly restricted proof model. The package replay checks the Lean file in the pinned project environment.
